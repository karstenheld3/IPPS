# KABOB Rules

Output standards for documents written `using KABOB` (anecdote, nut graf, body, kicker).

**Writing quality:** Apply `APAPALAN_RULES.md` and `MECT_WRITING_RULES.md`. Key rules: AP-PR-07 (be specific), MW-HS-01 (informative headings), MW-WC-10 (explicit over figurative).

## Rule Index

Structure (ST)
- KABOB-ST-01: Anecdote has at most 5 paragraphs
- KABOB-ST-02: Nut graf appears within the first 5 paragraphs
- KABOB-ST-03: Kicker returns to the anecdote subject or detail
- KABOB-ST-04: Body sections open with a claim

Content (CT)
- KABOB-CT-01: Anecdote contains only verified details
- KABOB-CT-02: Nut graf answers "what is this really about?" with one number
- KABOB-CT-03: Body alternates evidence types
- KABOB-CT-04: Kicker introduces no new argument

Evidence (EV)
- KABOB-EV-01: Anecdote representativeness is stated
- KABOB-EV-02: Body claims are sourced
- KABOB-EV-03: At least one counterpoint or limit is included

## KABOB-ST-01: Anecdote of at Most 5 Paragraphs

**BAD:** Seven paragraphs about one plant manager's morning before the reader learns the topic.

**GOOD:** Three paragraphs: the breakdown, the cost, the decision she faced.

## KABOB-ST-02: Nut Graf Within 5 Paragraphs

**BAD:** The trend is first named in paragraph 9.

**GOOD:** Paragraph 4: "Her plant is one of roughly 1,900 mid-sized manufacturers in the region that now face the same choice..."

## KABOB-ST-03: Kicker Returns to the Opening

**BAD:** The last paragraph summarizes market forecasts.

**GOOD:** The last paragraph returns to the plant manager and the pump that failed in the opening.

## KABOB-ST-04: Claim-First Body Sections

**BAD:** `## Background` followed by three paragraphs of history.

**GOOD:** `## Sensors are cheap; interpreting their data is not` followed by evidence.

## KABOB-CT-01: Verified Anecdote Details

**BAD:** "She stared out at the grey sky, wondering if her job was at risk." (no source for weather or thoughts)

**GOOD:** "She told us she had three hours to decide whether to stop the line."

## KABOB-CT-02: Nut Graf with Number

**BAD:** "Stories like this are becoming more common."

**GOOD:** "Unplanned downtime costs mid-sized manufacturers an estimated 3-5% of annual revenue, according to the regional industry association's 2026 survey."

## KABOB-CT-03: Alternating Evidence Types

**BAD:** Six consecutive paragraphs of survey statistics.

**GOOD:** Survey statistic, then an engineer's explanation, then a second short case, then analysis.

## KABOB-CT-04: No New Argument in the Kicker

**BAD:** "Meanwhile, the rise of humanoid robots may change everything."

**GOOD:** "Last month the same pump vibrated again. This time, the alert arrived nine days early."

## KABOB-EV-01: Representativeness Stated

**BAD:** The case is presented as typical without evidence.

**GOOD:** "Her plant's size and age match the median member of the association's survey."

## KABOB-EV-02: Sourced Body Claims

**BAD:** "Experts agree that predictive maintenance pays off."

**GOOD:** "In the association's survey of 312 plants, 41% of those using vibration sensors reported fewer unplanned stops after one year."

## KABOB-EV-03: Counterpoint Included

**BAD:** Only success stories appear.

**GOOD:** "Not every plant benefits: sites with fewer than 20 rotating machines rarely recover the sensor cost within three years."
