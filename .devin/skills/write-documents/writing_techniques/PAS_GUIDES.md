# PAS Guide

Read BEFORE writing a landing page, product page, direct-response email, paid ad, or launch announcement with `using PAS`. PAS = Problem, Agitate, Solve.

## 1. Decision: PAS or AIDA?

Use PAS when the reader is a prospect who must decide in seconds whether the offer is relevant, and the text ends with one action (sign up, book a call, buy).

Choose by reader awareness:
- **PAS**: Problem-aware readers who do not yet know your product (cold emails, ads, landing pages from search). Acknowledges pain first, agitates the cost of inaction, then introduces the solution mechanism.
- **AIDA**: Product-aware readers who already know the category (product pages, comparison pages, warm emails). If AIDA fits better, see [AIDA_GUIDES.md](AIDA_GUIDES.md).

Tell the user that a different technique fits better, and keep `PAS` until the user decides (`DRSYNART-DD-08`), when:
- The reader is an internal decision-maker evaluating a budget: use `MINTO`
- The reader wants a customer story with depth: use `KABOB`
- The reader is already a customer who needs instructions: use `DIATAXIS`

## 2. Plan Before Writing

1. Write the reader's problem in their own words, using the phrases customers use in support tickets, reviews, or interviews
2. Quantify the cost of the problem: time, money, risk, or frustration with a number (PAS-CT-03)
3. State the solution mechanism in one sentence: what the product does differently, not only what it is (PAS-CT-02)
4. Collect 1-2 proofs with baselines: customer results, benchmarks, or trial data (PAS-EV-01)
5. Write the honest limit: who should not buy, or what the product does not do (PAS-EV-02)
6. Choose one call to action (CTA): one verb, one destination (PAS-ST-02)

## 3. Execute Each Section

1. Headline: the outcome for the reader in their terms, ideally with a number (PAS-ST-03)
2. Problem: name the specific pain in 1-3 sentences (PAS-ST-01)
3. Agitate: show the cost of the status quo with a number; stay truthful, no fake urgency (PAS-CT-03)
4. Solve: the mechanism, then features translated into benefits (PAS-CT-02, PAS-CT-01, PAS-CT-04)
5. Proof: 1-2 quantified results with baseline and source (PAS-EV-01, PAS-EV-03)
6. Honest limit: one short paragraph on who this is not for (PAS-EV-02)
7. CTA: one button or link, one verb, repeated at most once at the end of a long page (PAS-ST-02)

## 4. When the User Also Passes a Persona

- This section applies only when the user passes `as <persona>`. Without it, apply no persona and do not pick one from the list below (`DRSYNART-DD-08`)
- Fitting personas, offered only when the user asks for persona help: `CONVERSION-COPYWRITER` (direct response), `PRODUCT-POSITIONER` (anti-hype B2B pages), `CASE-STORYTELLER` (proof sections)
- Warm personas (Warmth 6-7) fit cold audiences; mechanism-first personas (`PRODUCT-POSITIONER`) fit technical evaluators
- The honest limit is a technique element (PAS-EV-02); when the persona also has The Catch trait, use one honest-limit paragraph only, not both
- Persona style must not inflate proof numbers (PAS-EV-01), invent testimonials (PAS-EV-03), or add urgency that is not true (PAS-CT-03)

## 6. Strategic Review Questions

Used by `/critique` after writing:
- Does the opening acknowledge the prospect's specific pain in customer words before naming any product?
- Is the agitation backed by concrete cost or time figures rather than hype or artificial urgency?
- Would a skeptical reader trust the page more or less after reading the honest limit?
