# STA Guide

Read BEFORE writing an ebook chapter, practitioner guide, coaching guide, workshop handout, or onboarding course with `using STA`.

## 1. Decide Whether Show-Tell-Apply Fits

Use it when the reader wants to learn a reusable skill and will apply it to their own situation. The sequence is inductive: a concrete case (SHOW) creates recognition, an extracted principle (TELL) creates understanding, and numbered steps (APPLY) create capability. Typical outputs: nonfiction book chapters, paid ebooks, practitioner guides, coaching chapters, workshop materials.

Tell the user that a different technique fits better, and keep `STA` until the user decides (`DRSYNART-DD-08`), when:
- The reader needs one specific task done right now without learning a principle: use `DIATAXIS` (how-to mode)
- The reader must make a decision, not learn a skill: use `MINTO`
- The reader needs complete reference information: use `DIATAXIS` (reference mode)

## 2. Plan Before Writing

1. State the one principle the chapter teaches in one sentence. If you need two principles, plan two chapters or two units
2. Find the case: a real situation where ignoring the principle caused a visible failure or applying it caused a measurable success. Note the source or label it as composite
3. Derive the steps: 3-7 numbered actions the reader performs in their own context
4. Define the quick check: one observable test, under 15 minutes, that tells the reader the steps worked

## 3. Execute Each Section

1. SHOW: 3-5 sentences, one recognizable situation, concrete facts and numbers. No weather, no beverages, no invented dialogue
2. TELL: 1-2 sentences stating the principle in quotable form, directly after the case. Optional 1 paragraph explaining why it holds
3. APPLY: numbered steps, one action per step, each with the expected result where useful
4. Quick check: one sentence at the end of the unit
5. Chapter close: one paragraph linking back to the opening case and naming what the reader can now do

## 4. When the User Also Passes a Persona

- This section applies only when the user passes `as <persona>`. Without it, apply no persona and do not pick one from the list below (`DRSYNART-DD-08`)
- Fitting personas, offered only when the user asks for persona help: `BOOK-AUTHOR` (ebooks, books), `COACH-MENTOR` (leadership and habit guides), `TECHNICAL-WRITER` (practitioner guides), `PRACTITIONER-BLOGGER` (tutorial blog series)
- Persona trait The Colon Law fits the TELL sentence; One-Line Verification Step fits the quick check; Sub-Thought Callback can plant an image in SHOW and echo it in the chapter close. Use at most one trait per paragraph
- Coaching personas may acknowledge emotional barriers in SHOW, but APPLY still contains concrete actions
- Persona style must not turn a composite case into a real one or change its numbers

## 6. Strategic Review Questions

Used by `/critique` after writing:
- Does the case make the principle feel necessary, or would the reader accept the principle without it?
- Is the chapter teaching one principle, or several principles squeezed into one unit?
- Can a reader apply the steps in their own context without the author's specific tools?
