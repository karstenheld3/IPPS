# AIDA Guide

Read BEFORE writing a landing page, product page, direct-response email, paid ad, or launch announcement with `using AIDA`. AIDA = Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

## 1. Decision: AIDA or PAS?

Use AIDA when the reader is a prospect who already knows the product category, and the text builds interest in your specific approach and desire for your features, ending with one clear action (sign up, book a demo, start trial).

Choose by reader awareness:
- **AIDA**: Product-aware readers who already know the category (product pages, comparison pages, warm emails). Captures attention with a distinct angle, engages interest with mechanism details, and builds desire with translated benefits.
- **PAS**: Problem-aware readers who do not yet know your product and need their pain acknowledged first (cold emails, ads, search landing pages). If PAS fits better, see [PAS_GUIDES.md](PAS_GUIDES.md).

Tell the user that a different technique fits better, and keep `AIDA` until the user decides (`DRSYNART-DD-08`), when:
- The reader is an internal decision-maker evaluating a budget: use `MINTO`
- The reader wants a customer story with depth: use `KABOB`
- The reader is already a customer who needs instructions: use `DIATAXIS`

## 2. Plan Before Writing

1. Write the attention hook in reader terms: the distinct outcome or surprising insight (AIDA-ST-03)
2. Identify the interest angle: how the mechanism works differently from alternatives (AIDA-CT-01)
3. State the desire drivers: 2-4 key features translated into tangible reader benefits (AIDA-CT-02)
4. Collect 1-2 proofs with baselines: customer results, benchmarks, or trial data (AIDA-EV-01)
5. Write the honest limit: who should not buy, or what the product does not do (AIDA-EV-02)
6. Choose one call to action (CTA): one verb, one destination (AIDA-ST-02)

## 3. Execute Each Section

1. Headline (Attention): the outcome for the reader in their terms, ideally with a number (AIDA-ST-03, AIDA-ST-01)
2. Attention lead: capture the reader's attention with a concrete angle or contrast in 1-3 sentences (AIDA-ST-01)
3. Interest: explain the mechanism that makes the outcome possible; stay truthful, no hype (AIDA-CT-01, AIDA-CT-03)
4. Desire: translate features into reader benefits with quantified outcomes (AIDA-CT-02, AIDA-CT-04)
5. Proof: 1-2 quantified results with baseline and source (AIDA-EV-01, AIDA-EV-03)
6. Honest limit: one short paragraph on who this is not for (AIDA-EV-02)
7. Action (CTA): one button or link, one verb, repeated at most once at the end of a long page (AIDA-ST-02)

## 4. When the User Also Passes a Persona

- This section applies only when the user passes `as <persona>`. Without it, apply no persona and do not pick one from the list below (`DRSYNART-DD-08`)
- Fitting personas, offered only when the user asks for persona help: `CONVERSION-COPYWRITER` (direct response), `PRODUCT-POSITIONER` (anti-hype B2B pages), `CASE-STORYTELLER` (proof sections)
- Warm personas (Warmth 6-7) fit cold audiences; mechanism-first personas (`PRODUCT-POSITIONER`) fit technical evaluators
- The honest limit is a technique element (AIDA-EV-02); when the persona also has The Catch trait, use one honest-limit paragraph only, not both
- Persona style must not inflate proof numbers (AIDA-EV-01), invent testimonials (AIDA-EV-03), or add unverified claims (AIDA-CT-03)

## 6. Strategic Review Questions

Used by `/critique` after writing:
- Does the headline and lead immediately seize Attention without resorting to hype adjectives?
- Does the Interest and Desire progression show how the mechanism delivers reader benefits?
- Would a skeptical reader trust the page more or less after reading the honest limit?
