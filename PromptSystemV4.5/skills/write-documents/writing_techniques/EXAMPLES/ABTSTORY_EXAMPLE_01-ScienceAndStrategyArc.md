# ABTSTORY Example 01: ScienceAndStrategyArc

Public science-and-policy essay written `using ABTSTORY as INTELLECTUAL`. The city, survey, crews, and all figures are fictional.

## Context

A municipal research office wants a public essay explaining why the city changed its tree program from planting to watering. Readers are educated residents and council members who accept that trees cool streets but do not know why the program changed. The essay must hold their attention through survival statistics without becoming a report.

## Document

```markdown
# Why Riverton Stopped Counting Trees Planted

Street trees cool the pavement beneath them by up to 4 degrees Celsius on a summer afternoon, and for a decade the city of Riverton measured its climate program by one number: trees planted. But a municipal survey of 4,200 plantings from 2022 to 2025 found that 38% of new trees died within three years, long before their canopy was wide enough to cool anything. Therefore, Riverton now measures trees alive after five years, and it moved 22% of its planting budget into watering contracts.

## A young tree is a water debt, not an asset

A sapling arrives from the nursery with a root ball the size of a bucket. For its first three summers, it cannot reach groundwater; it lives on what crews and rain deliver. Arborists call the period *establishment* (from Latin *stabilis*, "standing firm"), and the word is accurate: until the roots spread, the tree is not yet standing on its own.

The survey shows how expensive that debt becomes when it is not paid. A tree planted for 450 EUR and then left unwatered had a 41% chance of dying by year three. A tree that received a scheduled watering visit every ten days during dry spells had a 9% chance.

Composite case based on three crew interviews: once, a crew planted 60 trees along a new tram line in April. Every day, the tram schedule kept them from parking a water truck on the street. Until one day in July, after 19 days without rain, a resident photographed the first dead tree. Because of that, the crew asked for a night watering slot. Until finally, the transit authority agreed, but only in September. Ever since, every planting permit on a transit corridor includes a watering access plan.

## Watering explains most deaths, but not all of them

Missed watering accounts for most of the losses, yet it is not the only cause. In 14% of dead trees, inspectors also found soil compacted by construction vehicles, which blocks roots even when water arrives. The survey cannot separate the two causes in those cases, so the new program funds both watering and soil loosening before planting.

## The number that matters is five years from now

The old metric rewarded the ceremony of planting. The new metric rewards the tree that is still alive when its shade finally reaches the pavement. Riverton plans fewer trees next year, 900 instead of 1,200, and expects more of them to be alive in 2031 than the last three programs combined delivered.
```

## Key Decisions

- One BUT in the opening (38% died within three years): the reader accepts the AND (trees cool streets), so the survival number creates the tension that carries the essay (ABTSTORY-ST-01, ABTSTORY-EV-01)
- THEREFORE stated as two concrete changes, the new metric and the 22% budget shift, instead of "we must rethink" (ABTSTORY-CT-02)
- Competing cause (soil compaction) given its own section: the evidence supports two causes, so the essay does not compress them into one (ABTSTORY-EV-02)
- Story Spine used once, for an embedded case, and labeled as composite (ABTSTORY-ST-03, ABTSTORY-EV-03)
- Persona `INTELLECTUAL` (Narrative 7, Technicality 3): the scientific term "establishment" is explained on first use with the persona's Etymological Root Gloss trait, the only trait in the essay
- Closing returns to the opening metric conflict (trees planted vs. trees alive) (ABTSTORY-ST-04)
