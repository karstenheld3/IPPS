# KABOB Guide

Read BEFORE writing a magazine feature, long-form blog feature, industry deep-dive, investigative feature, or narrative case study with `using KABOB`. The Wall Street Journal (WSJ) feature formula: anecdote, nut graf, analytical body, kicker that returns to the anecdote.

## 1. Decide Whether the Kabob Fits

Use it when an abstract trend, analysis, or investigation becomes understandable through one concrete person or organization, and the reader reads voluntarily. Typical outputs: industry features, newsletter deep-dives, investigative features, customer transformation stories, long-form blog features.

Tell the user that a different technique fits better, and keep `KABOB` until the user decides (`DRSYNART-DD-08`), when:
- The reader may stop early and needs the facts first: use `IMINTO`
- The reader is a decision-maker who wants the recommendation first: use `MINTO`
- No real, representative case exists: do not invent one; propose `ABTSTORY` with a factual opening

## 2. Plan Before Writing

1. Choose the anecdote subject: one real person or organization whose situation represents the trend. Confirm you can state why the case is typical, not exceptional
2. Write the nut graf first as 2-3 sentences: what this story is really about, why it matters now, and what the reader will learn
3. Plan the body as alternating evidence types: data, expert or document evidence, a second short case, analysis. Plan at least one counterpoint or limit
4. Choose the kicker detail now: a concrete detail from the anecdote that can return at the end with new meaning

## 3. Execute Each Section

1. Anecdote: 2-5 paragraphs. One person, one moment, verified details only. End it at the point of tension
2. Nut graf: immediately after the anecdote, within the first 5 paragraphs. Widen from the person to the trend with one number
3. Body: each section opens with a claim, then alternates evidence types. Return briefly to the anecdote subject once in the middle to keep the thread
4. Counterpoint: one section or paragraph that states where the trend does not hold or who disagrees, with evidence
5. Kicker: return to the anecdote subject and the planted detail. Show what changed or what remains open. No new argument

## 4. When the User Also Passes a Persona

- This section applies only when the user passes `as <persona>`. Without it, apply no persona and do not pick one from the list below (`DRSYNART-DD-08`)
- Fitting personas, offered only when the user asks for persona help: `SECTOR-COLUMNIST` (industry features), `INVESTIGATIVE-JOURNALIST` (accountability features), `CASE-STORYTELLER` (customer stories), `HISTORIAN` (historical features)
- High Narrative personas (6-8) fit naturally; low Narrative personas (2-3) should shorten the anecdote to 2 paragraphs
- Persona trait Sub-Thought Callback may reuse the kicker image; it counts as the persona's trait firing for that paragraph
- Persona style must not add scenic details that are not in the sources (weather, thoughts, dialogue)

## 6. Strategic Review Questions

Used by `/critique` after writing:
- Is the anecdote subject representative of the trend, or merely the most dramatic case found?
- Does the nut graf promise what the body actually delivers?
- Would the feature still convince if the anecdote were removed, or does the story carry claims the evidence does not?
